# AIA CI Plus

## Quick Summary

| Policy item | Study-source summary |
| --- | --- |
| Policy type | Critical-illness rider attached to an eligible AIA main policy |
| Critical-illness benefit | 100% of sum assured for one qualifying severe illness, subject to the definition |
| Death benefit | Source also lists 100% of sum assured on death; only one benefit is paid, and the rider ends after payment |
| Covered conditions | 44 conditions/treatments in 6 groups |
| Waiting period | 60 days; source notes accident-related death or TPD exceptions |
| Source-listed issue age | 15 days-75 years; renewal/coverage subject to age and main-policy term |
| Minimum sum assured | THB 100,000 in the source |
| Premium and cash value | Premium may rise with age; no cash value is listed if no claim is made |

## Claim Notes from the Study Source

- The source describes one 100% sum-assured payment for either a qualifying severe critical illness or death, whichever occurs first; the rider ends after payment. It is not hospital-expense reimbursement or daily hospital income.
- The source lists issue ages of 15 days-75 years, a minimum sum assured of THB 100,000, renewal through age 98, and coverage to age 99 or the end of the main policy, subject to the current plan rules.
- Premiums may increase by age band. The source describes possible AIA Vitality premium benefits up to 20% for eligible members; program terms change.
- Tax relief may apply to eligible premiums under current Revenue Department rules. Confirm the current limit and policy qualification.
- Key definition examples: invasive cancer excludes in-situ cancer, cervical CIN I-III, certain skin cancers, and early prostate cancer T1a/T1b; heart attack requires clinical, ECG, and enzyme evidence; CABG and valve surgery require qualifying operations; balloon angioplasty, stents, and TAVI are not the listed open-heart procedures.
- The source's stroke definition requires neurological symptoms lasting at least 24 hours and a remaining deficit after 6 weeks; TIA is excluded. Its coma definition cites at least 96 hours of life support and permanent brain damage.
- Other high-yield thresholds in the source include third-degree burns over at least 20% of body surface, and permanent disability lasting at least 6 months. The source lists assessment of 6 activities of daily living (ADLs), with inability to perform 3 or more, for some neurological conditions.
- The source lists exclusions including the 60-day waiting period, pre-existing conditions, suicide or intentional self-injury, and HIV except for specified blood-transfusion or occupational exposures. It also notes injury while unable to control oneself due to alcohol or drugs.
- Age limits vary by illness and product version. The source lists juvenile conditions before age 17 and cites different age cutoffs for Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and TPD in different passages.

Use the policy definitions and exclusions—not a short example list—to assess eligibility for a claim.
